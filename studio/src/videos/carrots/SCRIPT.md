# "Carrots" — a 10-second short (test piece)

Facts (from memory of: Smithsonian Magazine, "A WWII Propaganda Campaign Popularized the Myth That Carrots Help You
See in the Dark", 2013; Imperial War Museum on airborne interception radar and John "Cat's Eyes" Cunningham):
- In 1940 RAF night fighters began to carry airborne interception radar and started finding bombers in the dark.
- Britain's Ministry of Information and Ministry of Food credited the pilots' success to eating carrots (posters,
  "Dr Carrot"), which also helped to move a carrot surplus.
- There is no evidence either way that German intelligence believed it. The belief that carrots improve night
  vision is still common. (Vitamin A deficiency does harm night vision; extra carrots do not improve normal sight.)

## Voice-over (34 words, 10.3 s)
In nineteen forty, British pilots suddenly start shooting down bombers in the dark.
The secret is radar. So the government tells everyone it's carrots.
Nobody knows if Germany bought it. But your mum did.
