# "Late for Dinner" — how the Vikings found America by accident (script v1, 2026-11-26)

Facts (Grænlendinga saga, chapters 1-4, as translated in "The Vinland Sagas", Magnusson and Pálsson, Penguin;
Kuitems et al., "Evidence for European presence in the Americas in AD 1021", Nature 601, 2022):
- Bjarni Herjólfsson was a merchant who spent alternate winters with his father in Iceland. Around 986 he came home
  to find that his father Herjólf had sailed for Greenland with Erik the Red that summer.
- Bjarni decided to follow, although, as the saga has him say, none of them had ever sailed the Greenland Sea.
- After three days the land was out of sight, the wind dropped, then north winds and fog set in and for many days
  they did not know where they were.
- They sighted land: no mountains, well wooded, with low hills. Bjarni said it could not be Greenland (he had been
  told of its huge glaciers) and would not land. This happened with three lands in all; the crew wanted to go ashore
  for wood and water and he refused each time.
- The fourth land was Greenland, and they came ashore at the very headland where his father lived, Herjólfsnes.
  Bjarni gave up trading and stayed with his father.
- He was later criticised at the court of Earl Eirik in Norway for his lack of curiosity.
- Leif Eriksson bought Bjarni's ship, hired a crew of thirty-five, and retraced the route in reverse, landing and
  naming Helluland, Markland and Vinland, where they built houses. The saga gives no year; around 1000 is usual.
- The Norse site at L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland, was felled-wood dated to exactly 1021. Columbus: 1492.
- "No map, no compass" is fair: the magnetic compass reached Europe about two centuries later. The spoken lines,
  the note on the door, the signpost and the "for sale" sign are our jokes, not the saga. "Late for dinner" is ours.

## Voice-over (167 words, 47.0 s, 3.6 words/s)
The first European to see America didn't even get off the boat.
It's the year nine eighty-six, and a Viking trader named Bjarni just wants to spend the winter with his dad.
But his dad has moved. To Greenland. A place Bjarni has never seen.
No map. No compass. "How hard can it be?"
Three days out, the fog rolls in, and the wind shoves them south for days.
When it clears, there's land. Green hills, and forest right down to the sea.
The crew begs to go ashore. Bjarni takes one look. "No glaciers. Not Greenland. Keep sailing!"
He does this three times. Three new coasts. Zero footprints.
Then he finds Greenland, parks right in front of his dad's house, and never goes back.
You see, Bjarni wasn't exploring. He was late for dinner.
Years later, a man named Leif buys that exact boat, follows the story backwards, and walks ashore in America.
Five hundred years before Columbus. All because one guy got lost, and refused to stop.
