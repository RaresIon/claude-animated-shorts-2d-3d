# "The Rat Tail Problem" — script v1 (2026-11-26)

Facts (Michael G. Vann, "Of Rats, Rice, and Race: The Great Hanoi Rat Massacre, an Episode in French Colonial
History", French Colonial History 4, 2003; Vann and Clarke, "The Great Hanoi Rat Hunt", 2018):
- Hanoi, 1902, under French colonial rule. The new sewer system under the French quarter (over 14 km of it) was a
  showpiece of the colony, and it gave rats a protected network into the wealthy houses. Plague was the fear.
- The administration hired Vietnamese rat catchers to work the sewers. At the peak, in June 1902, daily counts
  passed 20,000 rats. The rat population did not noticeably fall.
- A bounty was then offered to the public: one cent for every rat tail handed in.
- Officials began to see live rats without tails in the streets: people cut the tail off and let the rat go to breed.
- Health inspectors found rat-raising operations on the outskirts of the city; rats were also brought in from
  outside to collect on.
- The bounty was cancelled.
- Signs in the film ("RAT HIGHWAY", "DEFINITELY NOT A RAT FARM") are our jokes, not history.

## Voice-over (160 words, 44.3 s, 3.6 words/s)
In nineteen-oh-two, the French paid the people of Hanoi for every rat they killed. And ended up with more rats.
The city had just built brand new sewers, the pride of the colony.
The rats loved them. A warm, dark highway, running under every fancy house.
So the government hired rat catchers. They killed twenty thousand rats a day. It wasn't enough.
New plan. Anyone who brings in a rat's tail, gets paid one cent.
And the tails pour in. Thousands of them. The officials are thrilled.
Then somebody notices something odd on the street. Rats. Alive. With no tails.
You see, a dead rat pays you once. But a rat with no tail goes home, and makes more rats. With more tails.
Then the inspectors find something worse, just outside town. Rat farms.
People were breeding rats, just to sell the tails.
So the French cancelled the reward. They had tried to buy fewer rats. And paid for more.
