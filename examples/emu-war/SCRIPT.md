# "The War I Won" — original script v1 (2026-11-25), told by an emu
Facts (Wikipedia "Emu War"; Robin, "The Flight of the Emu"; Meredith's reports as quoted in the press, 1932):
- After WWI, Australian ex-soldiers were given farmland in Western Australia (Campion district) and grew wheat.
- After the breeding season, ~20,000 emus migrated toward the coast and moved into the cleared, watered farmland:
  they ate and trampled the wheat and broke fences, letting rabbits in.
- The veterans asked the Minister of Defence, Sir George Pearce, for machine guns. He agreed.
- Major G.P.W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery), two soldiers, two Lewis guns, 10,000 rounds. Began 2 Nov 1932.
- 4 Nov: an ambush at a dam near ~1,000 emus; the gun jammed after about a dozen birds.
- A gun was mounted on a truck: the truck could not keep up with the birds and the ride was so rough the gunner could not fire.
- Emus split into small groups; observers reported each pack seemed to have its own leader, a tall bird keeping watch.
- Emus run up to ~50 km/h.
- By the end Meredith claimed 986 kills with 9,860 rounds: ten rounds per bird. Troops withdrawn 10 Dec 1932; the crop damage went on.
- Meredith: "If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds it would face any army in the world."

## Voice-over (~60 s)
In nineteen thirty-two, the Australian army declared war on me. And lost.
I'm an emu. Every year, twenty thousand of us walk toward the coast looking for food.
And this year, somebody has planted a buffet right in our way.
Miles of wheat, grown by old soldiers who had just become farmers.
We eat it, we trample it, and we kick holes in their fences, so the rabbits get in too.
So the farmers ask for machine guns. And the army actually sends them.
Two guns, ten thousand bullets, and one very serious Major Meredith.
On day three, they ambush a thousand of us at a dam.
They open fire, and the gun jams almost straight away.
So they bolt the gun onto a truck. Cute. I can run fifty kilometres an hour.
The truck can't keep up, and the ride is so bumpy the gunner can't hit a thing.
You see, we never move as one big flock. Every little gang keeps a tall lookout, and the second he spots a soldier, everybody runs a different way.
By the end, they had fired nearly ten thousand bullets. About ten for every bird they hit.
The Major said that with our bullet-carrying capacity, we could face any army in the world.
Then the army went home. We stayed. And the wheat? Still delicious.
